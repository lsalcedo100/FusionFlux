# Testing, CI and reproducibility

How the suite is organised, what CI enforces, and the separate question of
whether `results/` still follows from the raw data.

Run the test suite with:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q
```

or run the full lint/type/test gate with `make check` (`ruff check .`, then `mypy .`, then `pytest -q`).

The tests that back the reported results come first: `tests/test_hdb5.py` exercises the confinement pipeline against small in-memory frames, so it does not require the downloaded HDB5 dataset; `tests/test_dataset_integrity.py` covers the content pin, including each way it can fail, the guarantee that a corrupt download never lands at the target path, and that every generated JSON under `results/` carries the digest of the bytes it was computed from; `tests/test_scaling_law.py` checks the three hand-written least-squares solvers against a problem with a known closed-form answer and against each other, while `tests/test_solver_conditioning.py` measures that Cholesky's forward error grows at the square of the condition number where QR and SVD grow linearly, which is simultaneously a check that the implementations are what they claim to be; `tests/test_bootstrap_resolution.py` covers Result 2b, asserting the sharp version of the claim (the exponents that genuinely vary between machines widen under coarse resampling and the ones that do not, do not) rather than the blanket one; `tests/test_extrapolation.py` covers Result 4, including the assertion that no tree in the forest can output a value above its training range; `tests/test_flexibility_sweep.py` covers Result 4e, with its load-bearing test pinning the hand-rolled ridge path against the scikit-learn pipelines it replaces; `tests/test_size_extrapolation.py` covers Result 5, including that the ITER-matched cut is read off the data rather than hardcoded; and the four newest files each defend the one way their result could be silently fake. `tests/test_dimensional.py` attacks the Connor-Taylor derivation from three independent directions, since a sign error there would produce a constraint matrix that fits cleanly, obeys itself to machine precision and encodes physics nobody intended; the strongest of the three is that IPB98(y,2), published in 1999, lands on two of the derived surfaces to inside the rounding of its own exponents. `tests/test_conformal_shift.py` guards a claimed *repair*, which is easier to fake than a claimed failure because any interval covers everything if it is wide enough, so it checks for calibration leakage and that the widening is selective rather than a blanket inflation. `tests/test_replication.py` guards the row match: if matching against STD5 silently failed, every STD5 row would stay in the "disjoint" arm and Result 11 would reproduce Result 4 by construction, so the matcher is checked positively (STD5 against itself must overlap completely) as well as negatively, and the unit conversions are checked against the 4595 rows the two files share. `tests/test_forecast.py` cannot check a forecast against an answer that does not exist yet, so it checks the inputs instead, asserting that each device's parameter set reproduces the confinement time its own design paper quotes.

`tests/test_allometry.py` covers Result 13, and most of it guards the two ways that result could be silently wrong rather than loudly: the deposit is tab separated with carriage-return line endings, so a default read returns one row of 623 concatenated records instead of failing, and it marks missing measurements with `-9999`, which is a valid float that survives any null check and becomes a NaN only after the logarithm. Both are asserted directly, as is the pinning of Kleiber's exponent, since an unpinned "constrained" model is just the unconstrained one under another name.

`tests/test_scaling_audit.py` covers the domain-agnostic module, and does most of its work on a synthetic allometric problem rather than on tokamak data: a general tool tested only on the dataset it was extracted from is indistinguishable from a specialised one. `tests/test_summary_figure.py` binds the claims the summary figure makes in its own titles (that the best cross-validated model is the worst on a new machine, and that the ITER spread is 8.3x) to the artifacts it draws from.

The neutron-yield pipeline is covered by `tests/test_preprocessing.py`, `tests/test_training.py`, and `tests/test_inference.py`, with `tests/test_lawson.py` for the physics utility, shared fixtures in `tests/conftest.py`, and shared stubs/builders in `tests/helpers.py`. Between them they cover Lawson calculations, temperature conversions, preprocessing and validation rules, grouped-shot aggregation, training split behavior, training artifact cleanup, preprocessing-contract compatibility checks, negative prediction clipping, and single/batch inference edge cases.

`tests/test_predictor.py` and `tests/test_cli.py` cover the callable form of the study, where the risk differs from the analysis modules: those can be wrong and produce a visibly odd table, whereas `predictor.predict` can be wrong and produce a confident, plausible, well-formatted number for a machine nobody should be predicting yet, which is the exact failure the study is about. So they fix the behaviour that makes it a refusal rather than a calculator: that the training-ceiling check fires for ITER and not in distribution, that the recommendation stays on the model Result 8 selected when the query leaves the validated range, and that the numbers agree with `results/forecast.json` and the distance with `conformal_shift.row_mahalanobis`, so the tool and the prose cannot drift apart while both describe the same three machines. That cross-check earned its place immediately: it caught a card entry labelled `ridge_loglinear` that was actually the unpenalised fit on a different feature set.

`tests/test_scaling_audit.py` also covers the standalone distribution, and the interesting part is that it checks the package's *documentation*. Its README is the whole of what a reader outside this repository gets, and its examples were wrong when first written: they named `constraint_matrix`, `constraint_values` and `n_test_groups`, none of which exist, and `feature_mahalanobis` for a column called `mahalanobis`. So the README is now bound to the code the same way the study's prose is bound to its artifacts: every public name must appear in it, every documented column must come back from `audit_groups`, the constrained-fit snippet is executed rather than admired, and the five names that were wrong are asserted absent. The build tests alongside them assert that a wheel carries a module byte-identical to the root file, which is the package's central claim.

`tests/test_gp.py` and `tests/test_tree_allometry.py` cover Results 14 and 15, and in both the risk is the same: the experiment quietly stops being a controlled comparison. Result 14 rests on three Gaussian processes differing in one property, so the tests pin that the rungs share a noise model and starting values, that the linear rung still reproduces ridge, and that the bounded rung really does revert to the training mean far from the data rather than merely scoring badly. Result 15 rests on four feature counts over one row set under two splits, and the split is the part that went wrong once: grouping the cross-validation by species makes it a second extrapolation split, and run that way the reversal cannot appear at any dimension, which the analysis duly reported as a finding. `test_interpolation_split_keeps_every_species_on_both_sides` exists so that cannot recur.

`tests/test_packaging.py` and `tests/test_wheel_smoke.py` cover something no other suite here can see: what a stranger gets from `pip install fusionflux`, as opposed to what a contributor gets from a checkout. Every other test runs with the repository root on `sys.path` and `results/` on disk, and version 0.2.0's packaging was broken in two ways that were invisible under exactly those conditions. The wheel did not carry `results/predictor.json`, so the command the README opens with raised `FileNotFoundError` on every clean install; and it shipped each analysis script as a top-level module, so installing this project put `config`, `storage`, `validation` and `tools` into site-packages where they shadowed any other project's module of those names. Asserting against `pyproject.toml` would not have caught either one, because the metadata was self-consistent and wrong. So these suites build a real wheel, read it back, install it into a fresh virtualenv, and run `fusionflux predict` on ITER from a directory that is not this repository, checking that the recommendation and the refusal survive packaging and that the installed prediction matches the checkout's to floating-point equality. They are marked `slow` and deselected from the interpreter matrix with `-m "not slow"`, running instead in CI's own `wheel` job and in `make dist`.

`tests/test_reported_numbers.py` closes the loop between the analyses and the prose. Every headline figure in this README, `results/RESULTS.md`, the site template, `paper/paper.tex` and the committed `paper/paper.pdf` is typed by hand, so each one is bound there to the artifact field it came from: the test renders the artifact value, asserts it equals the literal, and asserts that literal is still present in the documents meant to carry it. It fails in both directions, so rerunning an analysis that moves a number fails just as loudly as editing a number in prose. It reads only committed files, so it needs neither the dataset nor a training run and runs in the ordinary suite. The PDF matters most here: it is the artifact people download, it is committed rather than built on demand, and it goes stale the moment `paper.tex` is corrected and not rebuilt. Reading it back with `pypdf` is the only check that catches that; a checkout without the `dev` extra skips it rather than failing.

`tests/test_compare_results.py` covers the comparator behind that gate, since a comparator that quietly passed everything would leave the workflow green while guarding nothing. `tests/test_release_metadata.py` does the same for `.zenodo.json`, which nothing else in the repository consumes and which Zenodo turns into a permanent record: it checks the fields Zenodo requires, that the author and licence agree with `CITATION.cff` and `LICENSE`, that the description still says the HDB5 data is third-party and not redistributed, and that no second copy of the file exists to drift from the real one. `tests/test_supported_python_versions.py` closes the same loop on the supported-Python claim, which had no artifact behind it and went stale exactly once: it treats `requires-python` as the source of truth and binds the CI matrix, the ruff target, the version ci.yml hardcodes to pick the constraints-pinned job, the README badge, and the four versions named in the paragraph below all back to it.

That leaves one gap: whether `results/` itself still follows from the raw data. The `reproduce` workflow answers it, on a weekly schedule, on demand, and whenever an analysis or its output changes on `main`. It fetches and verifies the dataset, runs `make results`, and fails if any committed CSV or JSON moved, uploading the regenerated directory so the diff can be read without rerunning it locally. Figures are excluded from that diff because matplotlib output is not byte-reproducible across versions; the numbers behind them are. Run the same check locally with `make reproduce`.

CI runs the same gate on Python 3.10–3.12 with coverage reporting (`pytest -q --cov --cov-report=term-missing`; the measured module list lives under `[tool.coverage.run]` in `pyproject.toml`, and `fail_under` holds the line at 77%, compared exactly rather than rounded to whole percent, which is what `precision = 2` beside it is for). Coverage is lowest in the `analysis_*.py` plotting and `main()` paths, which need the real datasets and are exercised by `reproduce` rather than by the suite, so that percentage is a floor on the dataset-free tests rather than a statement about the analyses. The floor sits just under what CI actually measures rather than well under it: 77.62% without any of the four deposits, which is CI's state and where 36 tests skip for want of a deposit or a trained artifact, against 78.17% on a full checkout. Compare the floor against the first number and never the second. The gap between them is about half a point, and a floor calibrated against a with-datasets figure once sat above anything CI could reach for several commits without going red, because coverage.py's default `precision = 0` passes any total that rounds up to the floor. Reproduce CI's condition in a fresh `git worktree`, which carries only the tracked synthetic CSV and so reproduces the runner's skip count and total exactly; do not move `data/` underneath a live run, and give concurrent `--cov` runs their own `COVERAGE_FILE`. No measured module branches on `sys.version_info`, so the three interpreters agree. A tighter floor catches a smaller regression, and there is no instability here to buy headroom against. The 3.10 job installs against `constraints.txt` to reproduce the tested training and artifact-loading environment, while the newer-Python jobs resolve current releases so the `>=3.10` support claim is actually exercised. Runs use pip caching and cancel superseded runs for the same ref, and Dependabot opens weekly grouped update PRs for the GitHub Actions and pip dependencies.

Two suites skip rather than fail on a fresh clone: `test_committed_artifact_manifest_supports_relocation` needs a locally trained artifact under `data/processed/` (gitignored), and the parts of `tests/test_dataset_integrity.py` that fingerprint the real STD5 file need it downloaded. The checks that matter most in that file do not, since they synthesise a mismatched file and assert on the refusal. `tests/test_dataset_provenance.py` applies that same discipline to the other three deposits and needs none of them: it synthesises a Figshare metabolic-rate table, a BAAD archive and a DB5.2.3 export, re-points each module's digest pin at what it wrote so the accepting branch is reachable at all, and covers the download, pin-check and parsing paths behind Results 11, 13 and 15 that CI could not measure before.
