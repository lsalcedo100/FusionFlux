Alternative text for the figures and tables of
"Holding out whole tokamaks reverses tree-ensemble versus power-law rankings
for energy-confinement scaling" (L. Salcedo), prepared to AIP Publishing's
request for a separate alt-text file. Supplementary figures carry their alt
text beneath their captions in the supplementary PDF.

FIGURE 1. Three stacked panels. Top: a slope chart of log-RMSE for four models
under two splits, held-out discharge on the left and held-out label on the
right. The random forest rises from 0.128 to 0.465 and the gradient booster
from 0.130 to 0.359, each with a wide vertical interval bar on the right; the
log-linear ridge power law moves from 0.181 to 0.214 and the analytic
IPB98(y,2) law from 0.199 to 0.188, so the two lines for the trees cross the
two nearly flat lines for the power laws. Middle: a scatter of per-label
log-RMSE against Mahalanobis distance from the training data on a log axis
from about 1.2 to 10, with the thirteen labels named. The random forest's
points rise with distance from about 0.3 at AUG to 0.86 at NSTX and 0.72 at
PBX-M, with a Spearman correlation of +0.85; the ridge points stay between
0.1 and 0.3 at every distance, correlation -0.06. JET is circled. Bottom: the
flexibility ladder, log-RMSE on a log axis against five models. For the
polynomial rungs of degree 1, 2 and 3 the median over labels rises slowly
from 0.22 to 0.29 while the worst label rises from 0.29 to 1.08 to 4.6. To the
right of a dotted divider the two tree ensembles sit at a median of about
0.3 to 0.45 and a worst label of 0.7 to 0.9, both circled.

FIGURE 2. Two stacked panels. Top: grouped bars of empirical coverage of
nominal 90 percent split-conformal intervals for five models under three
splits, grouped cross-validation, leave one label out, and the
ITER-size-matched cut, against a dashed line at 90 percent. Every model's
cross-validation bar sits at 90 or 91. IPB98(y,2) stays at 89 and 88 on the
other two splits and the ridge power law at 83 and 70; the bounded hybrid
falls to 64 and 76; the gradient booster falls to 45 and 0.3, and the random
forest to 35 and 3. Bottom: per-label coverage against Mahalanobis distance
on a log axis for three models under the held-out-label split, with the
thirteen label names in a gutter below the axis. The ridge line stays between
0.65 and 1.0 across all distances, correlation +0.27; the booster and forest
lines fall from about 0.5 near distance 1 to about 0.1 at distances above 5,
correlations -0.54 and -0.77.

FIGURE 3. Two stacked panels. Top: log-RMSE on held-out labels against the
size ratio each cut demands, from 1.1 to 5, for the unconstrained, Kadomtsev,
collisionless and electrostatic power laws and for the analytic IPB98(y,2)
law as a dashed black line. A dotted vertical line at 1.82 marks the
ITER-size-matched cut. Up to a ratio of about 2 the collisionless line is
lowest at about 0.18 to 0.2, the Kadomtsev line next at about 0.25, the
unconstrained line highest at about 0.28; beyond a ratio of 2.4 the points are
drawn faint and are excluded from claims. Bottom: a scatter of what each
constraint costs in sample (x, 0.18 to 0.225) against what it buys at the cut
(y, 0.18 to 0.28). The unconstrained fit sits at (0.181, 0.279), Kadomtsev at
(0.181, 0.254), collisionless at (0.182, 0.183) and electrostatic at
(0.2245, 0.237).

TABLE I. Six fitted models and the historical IPB98(y,2) law, with their
log-RMSE under discharge-grouped cross-validation and leave-one-label-out and
the ratio between the two. The rows are ordered by cross-validation score, and
the order under held-out labels is close to the reverse: the random forest
goes from 0.128 to 0.465 (ratio 3.6) and the log-linear ridge from 0.181 to
0.214 (ratio 1.2).

TABLE II. The random forest and the power law scored under four splits, each
pooled over rows and with each unit weighted equally. The forest leads under
both cross-validation rows and trails under both leave-one-out rows in all
eight cells.

TABLE III. Five control arms, each giving the cross-validation scores of the
forest and the power law, the number of held-out labels (of 13) and devices
(of 11) on which the forest is worse, and the mean paired gaps. The forest
loses on 13 and 11 with the nine engineering features, 9 and 9 with stored
energy as the target, 10 and 9 with the loss power deleted, 11 and 9 in the
dimensionless diagnostic, and 6 and 5 with within-device features only.

TABLE IV. Five models under three splits of increasing difficulty. Across the
ITER-size-matched cut the random forest scores 0.938 and the gradient booster
1.072, nearer the constant baseline's 1.459 than the power law's 0.278 and
IPB98(y,2)'s 0.194.

TABLE V. Empirical coverage of nominal 90 percent split-conformal intervals
for five models under cross-validation, held-out label and the size cut, with
the multiplicative half-width. The two tree ensembles fall from 91 percent
to 45 and 35 percent and then to 0.3 and 3 percent, at a half-width of 1.23
that does not change.

TABLE VI. The Connor-Taylor hierarchy of constrained power laws in the order
of the hierarchy, with each row's selection status, in-sample, cross-validated,
held-out-label and held-out-device scores, the count of devices on which it is
worse than the unconstrained fit, and its score at the ITER-size-matched cut.
The prospective Kadomtsev row improves the cut from 0.279 to 0.254 at no
in-sample cost; the exploratory collisionless row reaches 0.183.

TABLE VII. One RBF covariance family with three mean functions. A constant
mean scores 1.948 at the ITER-size-matched cut, a fitted power-law mean 0.278,
and IPB98(y,2)'s published exponents as the mean 0.186.
