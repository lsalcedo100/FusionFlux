Alternative text for the figures and tables of
"Holding out whole tokamaks reverses tree-ensemble versus power-law rankings
for energy-confinement scaling" (L. Salcedo), prepared to AIP Publishing's
request for a separate alt-text file. Supplementary figures and tables carry
their alt text beneath their captions in the supplementary PDF.

FIGURE 1. Three panels. A slope chart of log-RMSE under held-out discharges and
held-out labels, in which the two tree ensembles' lines rise steeply and the
two power laws' lines stay nearly level; a scatter of per-label error against
distance from the training data, rising for the random forest and level for
the ridge power law; and a flexibility ladder on which the worst-label error
rises with the degree of the polynomial.

FIGURE 2. Grouped bars of empirical coverage of nominal 90 percent intervals
under three splits. Every model's cross-validation bar sits near the nominal
line; the two tree ensembles' bars are far lower on a held-out label and lower
still at the size cut, while the linear laws' bars stay closer to it. Below,
per-label coverage against distance falls with distance for the two tree
ensembles and not for the ridge power law.

FIGURE 3. Top: held-out error against the size ratio each cut demands, for
the unconstrained power law, three constrained power laws and the historical
law; the collisionless line lies below the unconstrained line at every
well-powered cut, and cuts at larger ratios are drawn faint. Bottom: each
constraint's in-sample cost against its score at the size cut; the Kadomtsev
point sits at the unconstrained fit's cost and the collisionless point just
above it, both lower at the cut, and the electrostatic point at a higher cost.

TABLE I. Six fitted models and the historical IPB98(y,2) law under
discharge-grouped cross-validation and held-out labels. The random forest has
the lowest cross-validation error and the highest held-out-label error among
the three primary models; the relative order of the tree ensembles and the
log-linear fit changes between the two columns.

TABLE II. The random forest and the power law under four splits, pooled over
rows and with each unit weighted equally. The forest's error is the lower one
under both cross-validation rows and the higher one under both leave-one-out
rows in all eight cells.

TABLE III. Six control arms. The forest's error exceeds the power law's on
every label and device with the nine engineering features, on most of them
when stored energy is the target, when the loss power is deleted and in the
dimensionless diagnostic, on fewer than half with within-device features
only, and on about half with stored energy as the target on those features.

TABLE IV. Five models under three splits of increasing difficulty. At the size
cut the two tree ensembles' errors lie nearer the constant baseline's than the
power law's, while the historical law's error changes little across the three
splits.

TABLE V. Empirical coverage of nominal 90 percent split-conformal intervals.
The two tree ensembles cover between a third and a half of rows on a held-out
label and almost none at the size cut; the table lists one multiplicative
half-width per model.

TABLE VI. The Connor-Taylor hierarchy in order, with each row's selection
status. The prospective Kadomtsev constraint lowers the size-cut error at no
in-sample cost, the exploratory collisionless constraint lowers it most, and
the electrostatic constraint raises the in-sample and leave-one-out errors
above the unconstrained fit's.

TABLE VII. One RBF covariance family with three mean functions. The
constant-mean row has the highest error on every split; the row with
IPB98(y,2)'s published exponents as the mean has the lowest on every split.
