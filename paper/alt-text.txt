Alternative text for the figures and tables of
"Holding out whole tokamaks reverses tree-ensemble versus power-law rankings
for energy-confinement scaling" (L. Salcedo), prepared to AIP Publishing's
request for a separate alt-text file. Supplementary figures carry their alt
text beneath their captions in the supplementary PDF.

FIGURE 1. Three panels. The random forest's log-RMSE rises from 0.128 under
held-out discharges to 0.465 under held-out labels while the power law stays
near 0.2; the forest's per-label error rises with distance from the training
data and the power law's does not; worst-label error grows with flexibility.

FIGURE 2. Empirical coverage of nominal 90 percent intervals: every model
covers about 90 percent under cross-validation, but the two tree ensembles fall
to 45 and 35 percent on a held-out label and to 0.3 and 3 percent at the size
cut. Below, per-label coverage falls with distance for the trees only.

FIGURE 3. Top: held-out error against the size ratio each cut demands, with the
collisionless constraint lowest and the unconstrained fit highest up to a ratio
of 2. Bottom: each constraint's in-sample cost against its gain at the size
cut, where the collisionless rung reaches 0.183 at a cost of 0.001.

TABLE I. Six fitted models and the historical IPB98(y,2) law under
discharge-grouped cross-validation and held-out labels. The random forest is
best under cross-validation (0.128) and worst under held-out labels (0.465);
the power law moves only from 0.181 to 0.214.

TABLE II. The random forest and the power law under four splits, pooled over
rows and with each unit weighted equally. The forest leads under both
cross-validation rows and trails under both leave-one-out rows in all eight
cells.

TABLE III. Five control arms. The forest loses to the power law on 13 of 13
labels and 11 of 11 devices with the nine features, and still on 9 to 11
labels and 9 devices under the stored-energy, loss-power-deleted and
dimensionless arms; only within-device features remove the reversal.

TABLE IV. Five models under three splits of increasing difficulty. At the size
cut the random forest scores 0.938 and the gradient booster 1.072, nearer the
constant baseline's 1.459 than the power law's 0.278.

TABLE V. Empirical coverage of nominal 90 percent split-conformal intervals.
The two tree ensembles fall from 91 percent under cross-validation to 45 and
35 percent on a held-out label and to 0.3 and 3 percent at the size cut, at a
half-width that does not change.

TABLE VI. The Connor-Taylor hierarchy in order, with each row's selection
status. The prospective Kadomtsev constraint improves the size cut from 0.279
to 0.254 at no in-sample cost; the exploratory collisionless constraint
reaches 0.183.

TABLE VII. One RBF covariance family with three mean functions. At the size cut
a constant mean scores 1.948, a fitted power-law mean 0.278, and IPB98(y,2)'s
published exponents as the mean 0.186.
