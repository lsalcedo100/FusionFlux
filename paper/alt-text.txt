Alternative text for the figures and tables of
"Holding out whole tokamaks reverses tree-ensemble versus power-law rankings
for energy-confinement scaling" (L. Salcedo), prepared to AIP Publishing's
request for a separate alt-text file. Supplementary figures carry their alt
text beneath their captions in the supplementary PDF.

FIGURE 1. Three panels. The random forest's error more than triples when the
held-out unit changes from a discharge to a database label while the power
law's barely moves; the forest's per-label error rises with distance from the
training data and the power law's does not; and the worst-label error grows
with model flexibility.

FIGURE 2. Every model's nominal 90 percent interval covers about 90 percent
under cross-validation, but the two tree ensembles' coverage collapses on a
held-out label and almost vanishes at the size cut, while the power laws stay
well above half. Below, per-label coverage falls with distance for the trees
only.

FIGURE 3. Top: across the size-ordered sweep the collisionless constraint
gives the lowest held-out error and the unconstrained fit the highest, up to
the ratio where cuts become underpowered. Bottom: each constraint's in-sample
cost against its gain at the size cut; the collisionless rung gains most at
almost no cost.

TABLE I. Six fitted models and the historical IPB98(y,2) law under
discharge-grouped cross-validation and held-out labels. The random forest is
best under cross-validation and worst under held-out labels, and the two tree
ensembles trade places with the log-linear power law between the columns.

TABLE II. The random forest and the power law under four splits, pooled over
rows and with each unit weighted equally. The forest leads under both
cross-validation rows and trails under both leave-one-out rows in all eight
cells.

TABLE III. Five control arms. The forest loses to the power law on every
label and device with the nine engineering features, and still on most of
them when stored energy is the target, when the loss power is deleted and in
the dimensionless diagnostic; only restricting to within-device features
removes the reversal.

TABLE IV. Five models under three splits of increasing difficulty. At the size
cut the two tree ensembles score nearer the constant baseline than the power
law, while the historical law barely changes across the three splits.

TABLE V. Empirical coverage of nominal 90 percent split-conformal intervals.
The two tree ensembles cover about half of rows on a held-out label and
almost none at the size cut, at a half-width that does not change, so the
intervals give no sign of failing.

TABLE VI. The Connor-Taylor hierarchy in order, with each row's selection
status. The prospective Kadomtsev constraint improves the size cut at no
in-sample cost, and the exploratory collisionless constraint improves it
most; the electrostatic constraint costs accuracy everywhere.

TABLE VII. One RBF covariance family with three mean functions. A constant
mean fails badly at the size cut, a fitted power-law mean matches plain ridge
there, and IPB98(y,2)'s published exponents as the mean give the lowest error
on every split.
